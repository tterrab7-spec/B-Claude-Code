Virginia Natural Gas, Peninsula Headquarters Site Survey

Prepared for Virginia Natural Gas by CIVITAS CRES. This survey documents the three Newport News properties that best meet the location criteria VNG has expressed for a consolidated Peninsula headquarters, the screening that produced them, how each performs against those criteria, and the work underway to bring each under control. Owner outreach has begun on all three.

Market: Newport News, Virginia · Oyster Point and City Center submarketProgram: 10 to 15 acres, office and commercial zoning, within the City Center and Port Warwick amenity area

1. Client criteria

VNG has asked for a headquarters site that keeps the Peninsula team as close as possible to the City Center area of Newport News, near the restaurants and shopping of City Center at Oyster Point and Port Warwick, and near the company's existing Newport News office at 746 Diligence Drive. The site should be 8 acres at minimum and preferably 10 or more, carry office or commercial zoning so that a headquarters is a by-right use, and be non-residential in character. A site with an existing structure is acceptable if the land fits and the structure can be removed.

2. Screening summary

Twenty-seven parcels and tracts of 8 acres or more in Newport News and Hampton were identified from city and Economic Development Authority inventories, state site databases, brokerage listings, and owner-held land not on the market. Twenty-four were eliminated for one or more of the following reasons: residential zoning; distance from the City Center area (all Hampton sites, the airport corridor, and the northern end of Newport News); industrial park character unsuited to a headquarters address; waterfront brownfield condition requiring remediation; lease-only tenure on public or federal land; or an occupied improved parcel whose owner has no reason to sell. The three remaining sites are all in Newport News, all within about three miles of City Center, and all carry commercial zoning that permits a headquarters.

Assessment. The strongest match to VNG's criteria. A single vacant parcel, already zoned for office use, at a City Center address, within a mile of the existing office, with no demolition or remediation. The owner has held it idle for nearly a decade and has no announced plan for it, which supports either an outright purchase or a long-term ground lease; a carve-out that leaves the owner a future medical pad is available as a negotiating tool. Status: owner identified; outreach to the owner's finance and legal leadership has begun; parcel file (assessment, site plan history, wetland and resource protection buffers, utility stubs) ordered.

Due diligence items. Title and the 2016 deed; wetland delineation and Chesapeake Bay Preservation Area buffers; stormwater; geotechnical; traffic impact at City Center Boulevard; confirmation of C-2 permitted uses and any proffers from the earlier rezoning.

Municipal services in adjacent streets; extensions and phasing to be set in the city's master plan for the area

Adjacent to City Center; about 1 mile to 746 Diligence Drive; about 1.5 miles to Port Warwick

One of twelve priority sites the city marketed at its October 2025 development summit; the city is seeking proposals
Capacity
A 10 to 15 acre carve-out supports the same program as Site 1

Assessment. Equal to Site 1 on address, amenity and zoning, and the city has said publicly that it wants employment-generating development here. The difference is process and time: a public disposition runs through a proposal to the Economic Development Authority, conformance with the City Center master plan, and City Council approval, so 9 to 18 months to a signed agreement is realistic against 60 to 120 days with a private owner. Public ownership also opens the door to performance-based incentives that a private sale does not. Status: conversation opened with the Department of Development; request made for the summit site book and the city's preferred disposition structure.

Due diligence items. Master plan conformance and design standards; infrastructure phasing and cost sharing; parcel configuration and frontage; incentive eligibility; timeline to council action.

Assessment. The only other Newport News site with interstate access, commercial zoning and retail amenity in walking distance. It meets the acreage and zoning criteria and sits three miles up Jefferson Avenue rather than at City Center, so it ranks third on location. Its advantage is certainty of disposition: the owner has already told the city it will sell the remainder. Timing is the constraint; the owner is unlikely to transact before its store opens and the parcel split is recorded. Status: owner's real estate development group identified; outreach begun; letter of interest to be placed on file so VNG is first in line when the parcels are released.

Due diligence items. Final parcel configuration and acreage after the boundary line adjustment; cross-access and shared stormwater with the retail parcel; traffic improvements required under the owner's conditional use permit; confirmation of zoning district on the remainder.

Recommendation. Pursue Site 1 as the primary target and Site 2 in parallel as the public alternative. Running both keeps the private owner's price disciplined and gives VNG a fallback that the city has already said it wants to see developed as an employment anchor. Hold Site 3 as the third option with a letter of interest on file; it costs nothing to be first in line and it becomes the live alternative if City Center pricing moves beyond VNG's range.

8. Work in progress and next steps

Owner outreach, all three sites. Underway. Site 1 through the owner's finance and legal leadership; Site 2 through the Newport News Department of Development; Site 3 through the owner's eastern real estate development group.
Parcel files. Assessment, title, site plan and rezoning history, wetland and resource protection buffers, and utility stubs for Sites 1 and 3; master plan and infrastructure phasing for Site 2. Civil engineering partner engaged.
Program confirmation with VNG. Headcount to be housed, fleet and materials yard requirement, parking ratio, and any operations functions that would travel with the headquarters. This fixes the acreage carve-out for Site 2 and the building footprint for all three.
Letters of interest. Non-binding letters to the owners of Sites 1 and 3 within 30 days of program confirmation; formal expression of interest to the Economic Development Authority for Site 2 in the same window.
Incentive review. City of Newport News and Virginia Economic Development Partnership programs applicable to a headquarters relocation on each site, with Site 2's public-land structure evaluated separately.
Site visits. Walk all three sites with VNG facilities and operations leadership; a half day covers the three, which sit within a four-mile stretch of Jefferson Avenue and City Center Boulevard.

9. Basis of this survey

Parcel acreage, zoning and ownership are from City of Newport News assessor and planning records, City Council and Economic Development Authority actions, and recorded permit files. Distances are approximate driving distances. Building capacity figures assume two- to three-story office at a 0.25 to 0.35 floor area ratio with surface parking at 4 spaces per 1,000 square feet. Utility availability is stated from street frontage and prior use and must be confirmed with the utility providers. Nothing in this survey constitutes an offer, and no site is under contract.
